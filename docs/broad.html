Trick 5: Go broad

“1,850 brand-new freshmen… how are we teaching them to think about AI?”

The move. Six of the 22 outcomes need no GPU, no budget, and no programming prerequisite. That makes them teachable to the whole College of Engineering — or the whole university — this year:

• Specify a vague goal so a second team can test it without asking a question.
• Design a test suite that catches all seeded defects.
• Verify generated statistics, plots, and citations against primary sources.
• Benchmark an AI result against a non-AI baseline (20+ runs, effect size).
• Design an experiment with controls, confounds handled, and ablations.
• Learn the next tool from vendor documentation alone.

The vehicle. Launch CSC 202/301 as the college-wide freshman AI sequence. Service enrollment funds staff lines; degrees follow money. Sixteen of the 22 outcomes need no intensive compute at all, so the sequence runs on hardware we already own.

Why us. Every department will use AI. Only one studies its defects. Checking machines is the CS contribution to everyone else's curriculum — that is the service-course pitch in one sentence.

Cost. Two new course shells; no lab hardware. Highest-friction item in this deck below the credential moves — and it is the one the Provost's freshman quote asks for by name.

Combines with: 2 — the 22 skills is the source list; 7 — friction ladder places 202/301 in the sequence; 8 — teach past the bubble explains the no-GPU constraint as a feature.
