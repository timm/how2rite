Trick 8: Teach past the bubble

A curriculum built on judgment and parsimony survives a crash; one built on unlimited tokens does not.

The bet. Current AI economics will not hold. Token and GPU costs climb, vendors ration capacity, many AI products lose money per user. The “remove the human” camp rides a gold rush of subsidized tokens — perhaps to its detriment. If (when) the bubble bursts, the field moves to cost-effective neurosymbolic AI: mix and match LLMs with cheaper, faster methods.

The move. Own that era now. We will not win a GPU arms race — and neither will most employers, so our resource limits mirror theirs. Teach AI that does not require exponential compute:

• small models and bespoke tasks (the Provost's own C3);
• hybrid workflows: decompose, then allocate each sub-task to LLM / retrieval / optimization / deterministic logic, defending the split with measured cost and quality;
• rigorous evaluation on cheap hardware: baselines, repeats, effect sizes;
• shrink-to-fit: fixed CPU/memory budget, report the quality lost.

We are closer than we look. The grids show C3 (no GPU needed) scores 1–2 in nearly every subject we teach. Parsimony is already our default; 16 of the 22 outcomes need no intensive compute. The cheap-model agenda runs on hardware we own, today.

Either way we win. If the bubble holds, graduates who can price and check AI are more valuable, not less. If it bursts, we are the department that taught the post-bubble stack before anyone else offered it.

Cost. Framing plus the same course edits as trick 1. No hardware purchases — that is the point.

Combines with: 2 — the 22 skills (cost/scale outcomes); 4 — quote the Provost (C3, in print); 5 — go broad (no-GPU teaching at scale).
