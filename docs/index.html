AI at CSC

eight tricks — combine as you want

Tim Menzies · August 2026 · timm@ieee.org

This is not a plan. Plans are rigid; committees eat them. These are eight independent moves. Each works alone. Most work better in pairs. Pick the ones you like; ignore the rest. Each page below is a two-minute read.

Want AI in every course, with zero degree paperwork?
1. Redose — add one AI-assessment learning objective to courses that already run. Course-level edits. No committee.

Need to say what CS teaches that no one else does?
2. The 22 skills — twenty-two outcomes for assessing computation. Other departments use AI; only we can teach how to check it.

Which courses are closest to ideal? Prove it, don't argue it.
3. Score it — repertory grids score every subject against nine constructs, 1–5, with per-cell evidence. Gaps become visible, then fixable.

Need top-cover? The Provost already published the agenda.
4. Quote the Provost — every construct we score against comes from Provost Pfaendtner, in print. Nothing here asks leadership to believe anything new.

Want to teach 1,850 freshmen with no GPUs and no budget?
5. Go broad — six of the 22 outcomes need no GPU, no budget, no programming prerequisite. Teachable college-wide this year.

Want a vehicle that already runs?
6. Ride the SE concentration — the grid puts the Fall'26 SE courses closest to ideal (d=3–6; nothing else within 10). Route students through what exists.

Want credentials shipped this year, not next decade?
7. The friction ladder — rank moves by paperwork, cheapest first: course edits → MS+AI + freshman sequence → minor. Park the BS fight.

Worried the AI bubble bursts? Good — teach for it.
8. Teach past the bubble — small models, hybrid methods, rigorous eval on cheap hardware. A judgment curriculum survives the crash; a token curriculum does not.

Sample combos. Fast + cheap: 1+5.   Defensible: 3+4.   Ship something: 6+7.   The long game: 2+8.   The whole memo, reassembled: all eight.

Preliminary analysis; every score must be checked with the lecturer.
