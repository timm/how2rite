Trick 7: The friction ladder

Rank every move by paperwork. Climb from the bottom.

The move. Peers ship while we deliberate (Purdue, Arizona, Georgia Tech, Duke, Maryland — all have shipped AI credentials or core requirements; we have none, BS “on hold”). The answer is not one big proposal. It is a ladder, cheapest rung first, where each rung stands even if the ones above never happen:

Now — add AI-assessment learning objectives to existing courses (trick 1). Course-level edits; no degree paperwork.

This semester — land MS+AI (already near approval) and launch CSC 202/301 as the college-wide freshman sequence (trick 5). Service enrollment funds staff lines; degrees follow money.

Next — AI minor.

Parked — the BS. The “on hold” fight is not winnable this year, and everything above proceeds without it.

Why a ladder beats a plan. A plan fails when its slowest part stalls. A ladder banks each rung as it lands: the course edits pay off even if the minor dies in committee; the minor pays off even if the BS stays parked. Nothing waits on anything above it.

Industry pressure cuts both ways — our advisory board splits between “remove the human” and “hire problem solvers.” Both camps need graduates who can build and check agentic systems. Every rung of the ladder teaches that.

Cost. Sequencing only; the rungs are the other tricks.

Combines with: 1, 5, 6 are the rungs; 4 — quote the Provost (“being agile is essential”) is the top-cover.
