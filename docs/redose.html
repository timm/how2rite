Trick 1: Redose

“Where does the dose of AI come early on, and where do we keep redosing it?” — the Provost, in print.

The move. Add one AI-assessment learning objective to courses that already run. That is a course-level edit: the lecturer changes a syllabus line and one assignment. No degree paperwork, no new committee, no new hire.

Why it works. Our grid analysis (trick 3) shows a middle band of courses — 216, 230, 326, 405, 408, 415, 418, 474 — that are artifact-graded, GPU-free, and current on everything except AI. Each sits 10–13 points from the ideal, and almost all of that distance lives in two columns: no AI dose, no AI assessment. One edit per course moves each 4–6 points toward ideal.

The theory core is not the problem. 226, 333, 246, 316, 366 score a perfect 1 on fundamentals — the one thing the Provost defends against deskilling. They are not deficient; they are the redose targets. Same story at grad level (501, 505, 512).

Cost. Near zero. Friction: one conversation per lecturer.

Combines with: 2 — the 22 skills supplies the objectives to add; 3 — score it says which courses to edit first; 5 — go broad extends the same edit college-wide.
