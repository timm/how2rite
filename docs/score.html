Trick 3: Score it

Gaps you can see are gaps you can fix.

The move. Stop arguing about curriculum in the abstract. Score it. We built repertory grids: elements = subjects (or peer departments), constructs = nine criteria C1–C9 taken from the Provost's published positions, every cell scored 1 (ideal) to 5 with public evidence. FOCUS-style clustering then sorts rows and columns so the structure is visible at a glance.

Three grids so far. (i) Departments: NCSU vs the 12 BOG-approved peers plus Duke — Purdue closest to ideal, NCSU one point back. (ii) Undergrad subjects: the 21 courses our skills list cites, plus an IDEAL column. (iii) Grad subjects: same design, 16 courses.

What the grids showed.

• The proposed SE courses cluster with IDEAL (d=3–6); nothing else comes within 10 (trick 6).
• The AI-rigor column (test/eval/secure AI) is almost empty — in our catalog and across every peer. An open lane.
• The theory core scores “far from ideal” but perfect on fundamentals: not problems, redose targets (trick 1).
• Parsimony is already our default: nearly every subject teaches fine without GPUs (trick 8).

One methods lesson. We scored CSC 510 twice: from its catalog entry, distance ≈12; from its live Fall'26 repository, d=7. Score courses as taught, not as cataloged — so every cell must be checked with the lecturer before any of this leaves the building.

Cost. Tooling exists (cluster.py). Marginal cost of a new grid: an afternoon.

Combines with: 4 — quote the Provost supplies the constructs; 1 — redose and 6 — SE concentration act on what the grid reveals.
