\begin{abstract}
Decades of good advice on writing software engineering (SE) research papers~\cite{shaw03,widom06,williams11} share one flaw: none of it is executable, so none of it is checked.
This paper offers \textit{rite}: a fourteen-step writing loop, eight prompt divisions that encode the advice as machine-readable skills, and a small literature pipeline (search, knee detection, snowballing, keyword coding) over the OpenAlex index~\cite{priem22}.
One rule of rite is checkable and surprising: coding 54 papers on multi-objective explanation, only 22 had open-access full text, and their abstracts earned barely half the topic flags that their bodies earned.
Abstracts under-report their own papers; so rite requires that a paper's own title-plus-abstract, coded by its own pipeline, must match the coding of its full text.
This paper is written by that method and, per the method, critiques itself.
To install, clone github.com/timm/rite and run \texttt{etc/init.py}.
\end{abstract}
