All three are feed prose. Written for a reader who is deciding every two seconds whether to keep scrolling, so every device is there to retain attention rather than transfer information.

Tumblr/fanfic. Rhythm over content. Fragments as drumbeats, repetition for escalation, second person, italics for stress, present tense, the isolated line as gut punch. Intensity is the payload.

Medium. The explainer pose. Anecdote opening, header every two hundred words, numbered framework, bolded pull-quote mid-paragraph, aphorism to close each section. Twelve hundred words to deliver one claim.

LinkedIn. Performed sincerity. One sentence per paragraph, line breaks as pacing, a small reveal, "let that sink in," three lessons learned, closing question to farm replies.

The shared grammar is a promise-then-payoff loop at roughly paragraph scale. Set a hook, delay, resolve, reset. That is why the colon pivot, the trailing fragment, and the short paragraph all cluster together. They are the joints of the loop.

A paper has a captive reader who already committed at the abstract. So the retention machinery is not just wasted, it actively costs the reader time on the way to the number. Your house style is close to the inverse of all three: front-load the claim, uniform paragraph length, no hook, evidence where the payoff would go.
