\section{The method}\label{sec:method}

Rite splits into an engine and per-paper workdirs
(Figure~\ref{fig:sys}).
The engine is read-only at writing time; this is a trust boundary as
much as a convenience, since a tool that edits its own rules while
writing paper seven will eventually rot them.

The pipeline is disciplined by two single sources of truth.
The search goal and year range live only in the workdir README; the
coding vocabulary (topic flags and technology flags,
regular-expression families) lives only in the workdir
\texttt{flags.py}.
Retargeting the whole pipeline to a new paper therefore means editing
just two files.

The advice itself is small enough to print.
Every prompt in the engine obeys one format rule: a short quotable
form first, full detail after.
Figure~\ref{fig:prompts} reproduces the short forms of all eight
divisions verbatim; the engine's skills directory holds the detail
behind each line.
The figure is therefore the method at reading-glance resolution: a
referee who checks a submitted paper against these forty lines has
applied most of the checklist that produced it.

The structure division pins down the shape of the paper itself.
Figure~\ref{fig:bnf} states that shape as a definite clause grammar,
in the Prolog idiom where each head argument is a
\texttt{key=Variable} pair: the keys document which role each part
plays, and any part of a parsed paper can be pulled out by name.
The keywords earn their keep where one nonterminal is called twice in
a single rule (in \texttt{related}, backward and forward snowballing
are both just \texttt{cites}); there, position alone would be a silent
trap.

\begin{lstlisting}
paper(front=F, body=B, back=K) -->
    front(F), body(B), back(K).

front(title=T, author=A, abstract=X) -->
    title(T), author(A), abstract(X).

body(intro=I, related=R, method=M,
     results=E, discussion=D,
     conclusion=C) -->
    intro(I), related(R), method(M),
    results(E), discussion(D),
    conclusion(C).

intro(quote=Q, questions=Qs,
      contributions=Cs, faq=A,
      install=In, use=U, collab=V) -->
    quote(Q), rqs(Qs), contribs(Cs),
    faq(A), install(In), use(U),
    collab(V).

related(before=B, since=S) -->
    cites(B),  % backward snowball
    cites(S).  % forward snowball

discussion(observations=O, threats=T,
           future=W) -->
    observations(O), threats(T),
    future(W).

back(refs=Rs) --> refs(Rs).

The pipeline then runs from the workdir: search OpenAlex for the goal;
keep the papers above the knee of the sorted-citation curve;
backward-snowball their references for the shared classics; fetch
open-access PDFs; code abstracts, then code full texts.
Full-text coding counts a flag only above a frequency threshold (0.5
matches per 1000 words), a crude form of length-normalized term
weighting~\cite{salton88}; the cutoff value is ours and
Section~\ref{sec:disc} owes it a sensitivity analysis.

The loop's most opinionated gate is the abstract-consistency rule
(step 13): run the paper's own coding grid on its own title plus
abstract, and require every flag the body earns at threshold.
The justification is Section~\ref{sec:exhibit}: in the wild, abstracts
fail this test about half the time, and everyone who judges a paper
cheaply (bidding PC members, systematic-review coders, ranking
algorithms, skimming practitioners) reads only the abstract.

\subsection{The bench division: norms of the target field}
\label{sec:bench}

Seven divisions travel with the engine; the eighth is written fresh
for each paper.
The bench division records, in the workdir \texttt{bench.md}, the
benchmarking norms of the field the paper is aimed at: which datasets,
baselines, statistics, and reporting habits a referee there treats as
furniture.
The sources for this paper's instance are the ACM SIGSOFT Empirical
Standards (the Benchmarking, OptimizationStudies, and
SystematicReviews standards) plus the norms visible in the field's own
methodological papers.

For the target field of this method (software analytics and
search-based optimization), the recorded norms are as follows.
Datasets are public corpora cited by canonical name (the
PROMISE/SEACRAFT lineage for defect data, MOOT for multi-objective
tasks); results are reported per dataset with raw per-run numbers
persisted, since aggregate-only reporting is a named antipattern.
Every comparison carries a floor and a ceiling: the floor is random
search, mandatory for never-tackled problems, and the ceiling is the
strongest state of the art with a public, runnable implementation (the
standards list ``baseline unavailable'' as an invalid excuse).
Statistical practice is 20 or more repeats per dataset-treatment pair;
distributions, not means; significance and effect size demanded
conjunctively, never significance alone; Scott-Knott for ranking
multiple treatments.
Budgets, whether labels bought or wall-clock time, are stated before
results.
Shipping requires a replication package to the SIGSOFT artifact
standard, a construct-validity paragraph, and the inclusion of tasks
where the method loses.

Two properties make this division different from the other seven.
Firstly, it is per-paper by construction: the norms above would be
wrong for, say, a human-factors venue, hence the engine cannot carry
them.
Secondly, it is self-checking: any number in \texttt{bench.md} not yet
confirmed against the coded papers carries a \texttt{[verify]} mark,
and step 14 refuses to ship while one survives.
The division is therefore less a style guide than a contract with a
reviewer who has not been met yet.
