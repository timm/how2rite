\section{Reading In}
\label{sec:lit}

A topic chosen is a literature owed.
Our lit-review rules are few enough to fit in a box:
Figure~\ref{fig:prompt} shows the whole prompt, whose first three
lines are machine-read configuration and whose five rules drive the
pipeline.

\begin{figure}[t]
\fbox{\parbox{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule}{\small
{\ttfamily goal:~~human factors of AI assistants\\ \hspace*{4.2em}for
software engineering\\ years:~2021-2026\\ seed:~~(one anchor DOI per
wing)}\\[0.4em] Rules: (1)~define ``recent'' by field velocity: ten
years default, two to three for LLM-speed fields; (2)~search for the
goal in the year range, sort by citations, and read above the knee of
that curve (the point farthest from the first-to-last chord);
(3)~seeds join the reading set regardless of the knee; (4)~snowball
backward from the reading set for the classics, and forward from the
seeds for the newest work; (5)~the download rate is a finding: report
it, never silently drop missing papers.
Then code every abstract, and hunt for subsets and their overlaps.}}
\caption{The prompt that runs the literature review
(from the rite engine's search rules, github.com/timm/rite).}
\label{fig:prompt}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:lit} shows the rules running on the area that
\S\ref{sec:map} chose.
The knee cuts 400 fuzzy search hits down to 38 that demand attention;
the subset overlap cuts differently, to the 94 papers that both wings
of the team would call their own.
Note that the two cuts disagree, and that the disagreement is
informative.
The top of the cites curve is crowded with general AI blockbusters
that mention software engineering only in passing; the overlap region
is where our actual topic lives.
Raw search lies; hence the rules, and hence the human hours are spent
only after the machine has shrunk four hundred papers to dozens.
